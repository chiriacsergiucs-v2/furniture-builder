# Copy the planners' carcass, not their catalogue

The best "design it yourself" configurators share one architecture. They pair a parametric carcass on a preset size grid with a front/interior layout that the user edits, and they use **named starting configurations rather than a parameter-and-formula engine**. That means Mobila's catalogue should be a shelf of **self-contained design snapshots that insert by copy into its existing space tree**, not the earlier 32-template, 7-archetype, formula-string system. Every source family points the same way. IKEA (METOD, PAX, BESTÅ) sells ready "combination" articles that users then edit. Tylko shows about six global controls plus per-cell overrides. Professional tools such as Polyboard, Mozaik and imos store products as a division tree with copy-on-insert and keep construction and material standards separate. That is the model Mobila already committed to in ADR-0004, ADR-0005 and ADR-0011. The earlier proposal clashes with the codebase in four places. Its formula-string engine duplicates the space tree that ADR-0005 chose over a parameter catalogue. Three of its seven archetypes (framed chair, upholstered seat, slatted lounger) cannot be built from axis-aligned boxes. `extends` inheritance would let a template edit silently change saved cut lists. Its validation expressions sit inside templates, when pro tools keep rules in a registry keyed by part role. The revised catalogue below has **24 templates**: 10 kitchen, 8 storage, 2 desk and 4 deferred frame items. Kitchen templates get the most variants (up to 5), and a bookcase gets one. Each variant is marked as interior-only (it ships as a space-tree snapshot) or as needing new construction. **The web was blocked for all four research streams. Every dimension, count and vendor behaviour below is unverified model knowledge**, to be checked against manufacturer documents before it becomes a default.

## Four configurator families converge on "preset, then edit"

**Retail kitchen and wardrobe planners split a cabinet into separately sold parts on a discrete grid.** METOD separates the frame from fronts, hinges, drawers, legs and plinth. Its widths are 20/30/40/60/80 cm, base frames 80 cm high and 60 cm deep, wall frames 37 cm deep, and drawer fronts come in 10/20/40 cm modules that fill an 80 cm row ([ikea_and_kitchen_planners](research note: ikea_and_kitchen_planners)). PAX frames come in **50/75/100 cm widths, 35/58 cm depths and 201/236 cm heights**, with KOMPLEMENT interior parts sized to the frame (same note). The flow most worth copying is the one IKEA and PAX share. The user starts from a **ready-made combination article** (frame + fronts + fittings sold under one number), applies a front style to the whole kitchen in one step, and then overrides individual units. The most common user complaints are about **"glue parts" the list leaves out**: fillers, cover panels, plinth lengths and worktop joints. Next come confusing corner rules and the lack of non-standard sizes (same note). German manufacturers (Nobilia, Häcker, Schüller) work the same way at higher density. They use widths of 150–1200 mm, height grids such as Häcker's 13 cm "concept130", and plausibility rules shipped in the IDM Küche catalogue standard. Those rules block invalid option combinations and add mandatory accessories automatically (same note).

**DTC parametric brands expose very few controls and generate the rest.** Tylko shows roughly **width, height in rows, depth from 2–3 values, a style generator (Grid/Slant/Gradient/Pattern), density and colour**. Partitions, hardware and the panel list are computed, and doors or drawers come second as per-cell overrides ([parametric_dtc_configurators](research note: parametric_dtc_configurators)). Tylko's "TV stand", "bookcase" and "shoe rack" category pages open the same engine pre-loaded with a different starting configuration, so a catalogue entry is really a curated saved design (same note). Made-to-measure sites (deinSchrank, Schrankwerk) take millimetre-precise width, height and depth plus per-section interior. They open on a start screen of shape templates such as standard, sloped-ceiling, niche and corner (same note). The weak point of the generator approach is that **changing global parameters can reset or re-map per-cell edits**, because the cell grid changes underneath them (same note).

**Professional cabinet software stores products as parametric structure plus separate standards, and inserts by copy.** Polyboard, imos, Mozaik and Cabinet Vision all model the interior as a recursive **division/opening tree** with fixed or proportional sizes. Construction methods (joints, back type, overlaps) and material templates are separate layers that can be swapped job-wide ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). Variants are mostly **separate saved library items cloned from a base**. Insertion is **copy-on-insert**, with no automatic propagation from the library to existing jobs, while construction and material standards stay as live references (same note). Formula-per-part systems (Microvellum, SketchUp Dynamic Components) can express any construction, but they are **hard to author and break when global variables change**. Hinge counts, runner lengths and span limits live in **hardware and construction libraries, not in product templates**, and tools warn rather than block (same note). Casework tools largely skip chairs and sofas. Beds and tables appear as panel assemblies, and solid-wood frames need a linear-stock concept like OpenCutList's sheet/linear split (same note).

**Kitchen modules are mostly one carcass with different fronts.** About 80% of EU kitchen types are a single parametric carcass with a different front split and interior. These include door base, drawer base, drawer-over-door, wall door, open wall and pantry. Only **sink bases, appliance housings, dishwasher gaps, corners, lift-up flaps, hood units and pull-out larders** change the construction itself ([kitchen_modules_and_variants](research note: kitchen_modules_and_variants)). Sensible defaults are a **720 mm base carcass × 560 mm deep on a 100–150 mm plinth** (worktop about 900 mm), wall units 720 × 320–350 mm, tall units 2000–2200 mm, and widths from {150, 200, 300, 400, 450, 500, 600, 800, 900, 1000, 1200} (same note). Fillers, cover panels, plinths and worktops belong to the **run**, not to any single cabinet (same note). That fits ADR-0031's bay layout.

## The earlier 32-template proposal breaks five codebase decisions

Each critique below tests the earlier proposal against the authoritative ADRs and the new findings. What survives is listed at the end of the section.

**The formula-string part engine competes with the space tree.** The proposal derived part dimensions from strings such as `"W - 2*T"`, with parameter arrays and `extends`. In Mobila, one `buildModel()` already turns a product root plus a recursive space tree (empty/shelves/drawers/hanging/split, mm or fr sizes, doors on any space) into every view (ADR-0004). ADR-0005 explicitly **rejected a parameterised template catalogue as the core** and made templates starting points only. A second engine that computes parts from formulas would produce two sources of truth for geometry. It would also need an expression evaluator in an offline PWA, and it would hit the authoring and versioning brittleness that pro users report with Microvellum and SketchUp DC ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). The pro consensus is the opposite: a **division tree for layout, with a small declarative rule layer for exceptions** (same note). Mobila already has that tree. The verdict is to **drop the formula engine**. A template is a design snapshot, and its dimensions are resolved by the existing builders.

**Three of the seven archetypes cannot be built from boxes.** Framed chair, upholstered seat frame and slatted lounger all need angled backs, curved or raked legs, or upholstery volumes. ADR-0004 states that angled or shaped parts require a new panel kind. No casework tool handles upholstery beyond decorative blocks ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). Those archetypes, and every chair, sofa and lounger template built on them, should leave the catalogue until an ADR adds a shaped-panel kind. **Bed frame and framed table survive**, because legs, rails, slats and tops are boxes. They still need two things the codebase lacks: a new root per ADR-0007 (a fixed-topology frame, not a cabinet with a space tree) and a **sheet vs linear stock tag**, so that solid rails go to 1-D length optimisation rather than sheet nesting (same note). That makes them Phase 2. The "drawer carcass" archetype is not a separate archetype at all. It is a cabinet whose space tree contains drawers.

**`extends` inheritance would let catalogue edits rewrite saved designs.** Pro tools insert by copy and never propagate library edits into existing jobs automatically, because cut lists may already be in production ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). ADR-0011 already requires designs to be self-contained JSON, and the proposed ADR-0028 records catalogue origin as metadata. Inheritance between templates also adds little: the shared part is the product root and builder (ADR-0007), which is code, not template data. The fix is **copy-on-insert with `origin {templateId, variantId, catalogVersion}` as provenance only**. A later "template updated, review changes" action can offer an explicit re-apply, but never a silent one.

**"Variants as parameter overrides" assumes a generator Mobila does not have.** Overrides only work if something regenerates the structure from parameters, which is the Tylko model, and that model has the override-loss problem ([parametric_dtc_configurators](research note: parametric_dtc_configurators)). IKEA's combination articles and Mozaik's sibling items show the simpler route: a **variant is a complete stored configuration**. For Mobila that means a full space-tree snapshot, plus front assignments and any root flags. A useful side effect is that interior-only variants become **reusable interior presets**. "3 drawers, 1 low + 2 high" can be applied to a drawer base, a chest or a nightstand, and the action is undoable (IKEA note).

**Validation expressions belong in a rules registry that emits diagnostics, not in templates.** Hinge-count-by-height, runner snap, shelf span and part-exceeds-sheet checks are properties of a part role and its hardware or material profile, so pro tools keep them in libraries ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). ADR-0008 already makes problems diagnostics rather than exceptions. Templates should carry only **catalogue metadata**: default dimensions, a sensible min/max range and a width grid (the 2020/SKU pattern, same note). A registry also means user-built designs get the same checks as catalogue ones.

**Two smaller corrections apply.** On grouping, DTC sites group by **product type first, with room as a secondary filter**, and implement it as tags so the same item can appear in several rooms ([parametric_dtc_configurators](research note: parametric_dtc_configurators)). A wardrobe belongs to the bedroom and the hallway, and a bench to the hallway and the dining room. The default view should therefore be by function, with room as a tag filter, which matches ADR-0027's category/brand/saved structure. On coverage, the earlier proposal spread its 32 slots across seating, sleeping and tables and **under-served the kitchen**. The kitchen is where retail planners carry 15–20 base and 8–10 wall variants, and where most cut-list value sits ([ikea_and_kitchen_planners](research note: ikea_and_kitchen_planners)). The proposal also left out the run-level glue parts that users complain about most.

**Hardware-dependent variants must not ship with invented specs.** Lift-up flaps (Aventos-class), carousels, U-cut sink drawers, gas-strut beds, tip-out shoe pivots and waste pull-outs all depend on vendor sizing tables, which the notes flag as unverified ([kitchen_modules_and_variants](research note: kitchen_modules_and_variants)). Under ADR-0010 such variants can ship only as geometry, with a **"hardware: user-specified" diagnostic**, or wait until verified hardware data exists.

**Several parts of the earlier proposal should stay.** These are a JSON, data-driven catalogue bundled with the static PWA (ADR-0002); min/max ranges and enumerated presets, demoted to catalogue metadata; the side-panel editor; a grouping toggle; the idea of named variants; and the room/function taxonomy, as tags.

## Ship snapshots, grids and run-level glue parts

The revised design has three layers, mirroring the pro tools' split between structure, standards and catalogue ([pro_cabinet_software](research note: pro_cabinet_software)). **Structure** is the existing product roots and space tree, untouched. **Standards** are project-level material and construction choices: carcass board, front material, back panel and edge band, cascading from project to product to space, as the IKEA "apply style to all" pattern and Cabinet Vision's job → room → cabinet inheritance do. **Catalogue** is a data file of families. Each family entry holds `id`, `title`, `root` (cabinet/desk/corner, plus future bed/table), `tags {function, rooms[]}`, `dims {default, min, max, widthGrid[]}`, `catalogVersion`, and `variants[1..5]`. Each variant has `id`, `title`, `kind: "interior" | "construction"`, `requires[]` (the new capabilities it depends on) and `design`, a complete self-contained design per ADR-0011. Inserting a variant deep-copies `design`, applies the chosen dimensions through the root builder (fr-sized spaces redistribute and mm-sized ones hold), and writes `origin` per ADR-0028. Variants whose `requires[]` is not yet implemented are hidden or badged rather than half-working.

Four behaviours from the research should come with the catalogue. The first is **grid defaults with a free-size override**: offer the EU width set as chips and accept any integer millimetre value with a diagnostic, since Mobila cuts to order and IKEA cannot ([ikea_and_kitchen_planners](research note: ikea_and_kitchen_planners)). The second is **"apply interior preset"** on any compatible carcass, as an undoable action. The third is **run-level generated parts** in ADR-0031's bay layout: fillers (with a scribe allowance), cover panels at exposed ends, plinth by run length, and optionally worktop panels. Each should appear in the cut list with a reason, because those are the parts retail lists drop (same note). The fourth is **auto-fill a wall run** from the width grid, with the remainder becoming a filler. The notes flag this as a likely differentiator, but it is unverified whether IKEA offers it (same note). A Tylko-style "density" generator for shelving can come later as a code-level function that emits a space tree. It would be a new ADR, not a formula language ([parametric_dtc_configurators](research note: parametric_dtc_configurators)).

The variant counts follow one rule: **ship a variant only where the edit is not obvious, or where the construction differs**. Simple items get one or two variants, and kitchen cabinets get up to five. Retail planners carry dozens of kitchen types because their fronts are fixed SKUs. In Mobila, a user can turn "2 doors" into "1 door" in two clicks.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the catalogue is for. It is not a product-definition language. It is a **library of good first moves into an editor that already exists**, and the earlier proposal's strongest machinery (formulas, inheritance, embedded validation) was rebuilding, less safely, decisions the codebase had already settled. The real gaps the research exposes are elsewhere: **run-level derived parts** (fillers, cover panels, plinth), a **rules registry** that turns hardware and material limits into diagnostics, a handful of **construction flags** (omit top or back, open niche, front without carcass) that unlock about 10 of the 15 construction-dependent kitchen variants, and a **linear-stock tag** that opens the door to beds and tables. These should be prioritised over adding more templates.

Two uncertainties should gate implementation. First, every dimension and grid value in this report must be checked against current METOD/PAX buying guides, EN 1116 and appliance installation drawings before it becomes a default. Second, no hardware-dependent variant (flaps, carousels, U-cut drawers, gas struts, sliding tracks) should ship until verified vendor data exists, because ADR-0010 forbids defaulting those specs.
