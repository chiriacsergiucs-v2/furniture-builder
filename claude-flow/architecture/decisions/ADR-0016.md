## Context

Resizing a cabinet must resize its shelves and drawers deterministically, without touching unrelated instances, and
editing one bay of a split cabinet must not renumber the other bay's parts (presets and projects pin part identity).

## Decision

Instances are nodes with their own stable IDs, a named parent slot (e.g. `interior.main`, `front.main`) and
`ParentBinding`s that read **only the immediate parent's resolved values**. Resolution rejects incompatible slot
children, duplicate IDs, unsupported definition versions, slot capacity violations and cycles. The five
composition operations are pure functions returning new trees: `insertComponent`, `replaceComponent`,
`splitCompartment`, `repeatComponents` (preserving IDs at unchanged logical indices) and `connectModules`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Global constraint solver** — rejected: overkill and non-deterministic ordering; one-way binding is enough for casework.
- **Mutable tree edits** — rejected: breaks preset copy-on-write and makes undo/compare hard.

## Consequences

- Child-to-parent or sibling references are not allowed; a feature needing them must go through architecture-authority.
- `connectModules` is deliberately limited to two same-height/depth/thickness carcasses docked on the right; arbitrary 3D placement is out of scope.

Unit test: change parent width → child shelf width changes, sibling cabinet unchanged; repeat N→N+1 keeps the first N child IDs.
